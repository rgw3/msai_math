# MSAI Math Reference

Two companion glossaries built for students in the University of Texas CDSO Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence (MSAI) program: one for notation, one for concepts.

## Contents

- **[math_symbols.md](math_symbols.md)** — every mathematical *symbol* and notation convention encountered across the program's coursework (calculus, linear algebra, probability, logic, set theory), alphabetized and defined. 165 entries.
- **[math_concepts.md](math_concepts.md)** — every AI/ML and mathematical *concept* — ideas and techniques that don't have one dedicated symbol of their own, as opposed to notation — spanning calculus, linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimization, and classical AI. 139 entries.

## Who this is for

This is written for MSAI students who are strong, capable learners moving into a technical graduate program from a non-quantitative background, or returning to math after time away from it — rather than students who lack ability. The program's coursework introduces notation and concepts from calculus, linear algebra, probability, logic, statistics, optimization, and classical AI quickly, and assumes fluency with them. These documents exist to close that specific gap.

Every entry is written to stand on its own: it starts from what a typical Algebra 2 background already covers — functions, graphing, exponents, systems of equations, if-then reasoning — and builds outward from there, rather than assuming prior exposure to the more advanced math or ML/AI background a typical undergraduate STEM or CS degree would have included.

## How entries are structured

Each entry generally includes:

- **The Big Idea** — how the symbol or concept connects back to something from Algebra 2, or to another entry already covered.
- **General Usage** — a plain-language definition, independent of any one field.
- **AI/ML Usage** — how it specifically shows up in the MSAI curriculum.

Symbol entries also note how to type or produce the character (e.g. keyboard shortcuts on macOS) where relevant.

## How to use this

Both files are alphabetized with a three-column jump table at the top — read down the first column, then the second, then the third — so you can scan for a symbol or term you don't recognize and jump straight to its definition. `math_concepts.md` cross-references `math_symbols.md` (and vice versa) wherever a concept builds on notation, so it's worth having both open side by side.

## Contributing

Found a symbol or concept from lecture that isn't covered, or an explanation that's unclear? Open an issue or a pull request — additions should follow the existing entry format (Big Idea / General Usage / AI/ML Usage) and start from an Algebra 2 baseline rather than assuming prior exposure.
